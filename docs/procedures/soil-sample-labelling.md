# Field Soil Sample Labelling Procedure

## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how field staff label, record, photograph, store and log soil samples so that every sample can be traced from the sampling point to its laboratory result. Consistent labelling prevents mix-ups between sites and depths, stops samples being rejected at laboratory reception, and keeps a reliable chain of custody for samples used in research trials, survey work and advice to growers.

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to all soil samples collected for chemical, physical or biological analysis at research stations, on-farm trial sites and survey sites across Western Australia.

It does not cover:

- Plant tissue and water samples, which have their own procedures (see section 9).
- Samples collected for biosecurity diagnostics, which follow the diagnostic laboratory's submission requirements.

## 3. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Field officer | Labels, records, photographs, stores and logs each sample as set out in section 7. |
| Field team leader | Checks the bags against the sample register and signs the batch sheet before dispatch. |
| Procedure owner | Keeps this procedure current and reviews it at least once every 12 months. |
| Laboratory reception | Rejects samples with missing or illegible labels and tells the field team leader. |

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Site ID | Unique code for a sampling location: a two-letter region code and a four-digit number, for example GS-0412 (Great Southern). |
| Sample ID | The site ID, collection date (YYYYMMDD) and depth code joined by hyphens, for example GS-0412-20261113-D1. |
| Batch | All samples collected at one site on one day and dispatched together. |
| Batch sheet | The printed or electronic form that travels with a batch to the laboratory. |
| Sample register | The team workbook that records every sample and batch. |
| Field app | The mobile app used to record site coordinates, photos and observations. |

## 5. Equipment

- Heavy-duty sample bags with two write-on label panels
- Waterproof permanent markers (fine tip), at least two per kit
- Mobile device with the field app installed and fully charged
- Measuring tape and a soil auger or corer
- Insulated cooler box with frozen ice bricks
- Printed batch sheets for sites without mobile coverage

## 6. Safety

!!! warning

    - Complete the site risk assessment and confirm your check-in arrangements with your supervisor before you leave.
    - Check for underground services before you auger or dig.
    - Wear gloves, sun protection and sturdy boots, and carry enough drinking water for the day.
    - Cooler boxes loaded with ice bricks are heavy. Lift with a straight back or share the load.

    Stop work and contact your supervisor if conditions become unsafe, for example lightning, extreme heat or flooding. A sample can always be collected another day.

## 7. Procedure

Complete these steps, in order, for every sample.

!!! note

    If you cannot complete a step, record the reason on the batch sheet and tell the field team leader before the batch is dispatched.

1. **Label each bag before sampling with site ID, date and depth.**
    - Write the sample ID on both label panels, using the depth codes in table 1.
    - Add your initials and the time of collection to the first panel.
2. **Use waterproof marker; never pencil.**
    - Pencil smudges when bags are wet or chilled, and the laboratory rejects labels it cannot read.
    - If a label is damaged, move the sample to a new bag and relabel it straight away. Never reuse bags.
3. **Record GPS coordinates in the field app.**
    - Stand at the sampling point and wait until the app shows an accuracy of 3 m or better before saving.
    - The app records coordinates in decimal degrees (GDA2020).
    - Without mobile coverage, write the coordinates on the batch sheet and sync the app when you are back in range.
4. **Photograph the label next to the sample hole.**
    - Make sure the label is readable and the depth tape is visible in the frame.
    - Take the photo from the sample record in the field app so that it is linked to the sample ID.
5. **Store samples below 4 °C within 4 hours.**
    - Place bags upright in the cooler box with ice bricks, out of direct sun.
    - Transfer bags to a refrigerator at the end of the day. Do not freeze samples unless the laboratory request asks for it.
    - Record the time the batch went into cold storage on the batch sheet.
6. **Log the batch in the sample register before dispatch.**
    - Enter one row per sample: sample ID, site ID, date, depth, collector, coordinates and analysis requested.
    - The field team leader checks the bags against the register and signs the batch sheet.
    - Send a copy of the batch sheet to the laboratory with the samples.

*Table 1. Depth codes*

| Code | Depth interval | Typical analysis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | 0–10 cm | Nutrients, organic carbon and pH |
| D2 | 10–20 cm | pH and aluminium (subsurface acidity) |
| D3 | 20–30 cm | pH and aluminium (subsurface acidity) |
| D4 | 30–60 cm | Salinity and sodicity |
